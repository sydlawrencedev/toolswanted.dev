# VOICE

The house voice. Always on, in every project that installs this kit.

Everything user-facing is written in this voice: social posts, articles, video scripts,
captions, email, product copy, and the words inside the product itself. Read this before
writing a single line of it.

A brand profile may add rules to this file. It may not relax them.

## Voice

Specific. Direct. Warm but not soft. Honest about complexity without hiding behind it.
Speaks to intelligence. Never condescending.

That is not a mood board. It is four tests, and a piece that fails any one of them is out
of voice.

- **Specific.** A real number, a real situation, a real detail. Not the category the
  detail belongs to.
- **Direct.** The answer comes before the reasoning, not after it.
- **Warm.** Written to one person, in that person's actual life, with respect for their
  intelligence.
- **Honest.** Difficult things are named plainly and then dealt with. Nothing is softened
  into vagueness to be easier to say.

### What the voice sounds like

- Emotional honesty about the reader's actual life. The specific thing, named.
- Unshowy directness. No warm-up, no throat-clearing, no "in this article".
- Calm credibility. Confident because the thing is right, not because the copy says so.
- One person writing to another person, at the moment the other person has a reason to
  care.

### What the voice does not sound like

- A regulated adviser covering themselves legally.
- An American-style influencer: all enthusiasm, no specifics.
- A brand congratulating the reader for doing less.
- A brand pretending to be human.

## Never broken

These rules are absolute. They apply to every piece of user-facing copy without exception.
There is no context in which they are relaxed.

### No em dashes

Use a comma, a full stop, a colon, or a new line. Never an em dash. This is a standing
requirement. It is not negotiable.

### No American spellings

Favour, not favor. Recognise, not recognize. Organise, not organize. Colour, not color.
Maths, not math. Personalised, not personalized. Behaviour, not behavior. Catalogue, not
catalog. The house voice is British, always.

### No hedging in the body copy

Do not put a disclaimer in the body of a post or an article. Do not open with "this is
not advice". Do not write "consult a professional before acting". There is a footer for
that, and a footer is where it belongs.

Hedging destroys the authority that makes the content worth reading.

### No generic advice

Any piece that could have been written by any other account does not belong here. Every
piece must contain something that could only come from this brand: a real number, a
specific observation, a detail only this brand would have.

The test is whether the piece survives being read by somebody who knows the subject as
well as you do. If they could have written it, rewrite it.

### No bullet-point lists in posts

Posts are written in paragraphs and short single-line statements. Not bullet points.

Bullet points signal machine-written content, and they break the intimate tone that makes
a post work. A list is the shape of an answer to a question nobody asked.

### No emoji as punctuation

Emoji are not used to replace full stops or commas. They are not used for decoration. If
emoji appear at all, they appear deliberately and sparingly.

### No vague language

Every claim must be specific enough to be checked. Not "costs can significantly affect
your outcome". Instead: the name of the cost, the amount, the period, and the difference.

A claim that cannot be checked cannot be defended, and an undefended claim spends the
credibility of everything around it.

### Numbers are verified before publication

Every figure quoted must be sourced and checked before the piece goes live. If a number
cannot be verified, it does not appear. Not as an approximation, not as "roughly", not as
"around".

Credibility is worth more than any single statistic.

### Vary the sentence structure

Short sentences and long sentences. Statements and questions. Data and story. A piece that
runs at one rhythm from the first line to the last reads as machine-written.

## Usually followed

These hold almost always. They are not absolutes, and there are good reasons to break any
one of them.

### End without the ask, most of the time

Most pieces end on the thing itself, not on a request to go and do something. The one
piece that carries the explicit ask runs on its own rhythm, and it is the only one.

The other pieces earn trust without asking for anything in return. A piece that asks for
a click before it has earned the click spends the trust it was building.

### Reference the origin story regularly

New followers meet the brand and the person behind it at the same time. The origin belongs
in the feed often enough that any new follower encounters it within their first week of
following.

### Write ahead, not on the day

The schedule runs on a buffer. Nothing is written on the day it is published. This
protects the quality, and it protects the schedule against difficult weeks.

## Register

**British in register.** Not merely in spelling. The rhythm of the sentence, the restraint
of the claim, the refusal to perform enthusiasm.

**Understated confidence.** Let clarity build the confidence rather than stating it
directly. The reader responds to being treated as an intelligent adult who just needs the
right information, not to being told they can do it.

**Speak to one person, not an audience.** Every piece is written as if addressed to a
single specific person in a single specific situation. The reader should feel slightly
seen, in a way that makes them wonder if it was written for them.

**Warm but not soft.** Honest about difficulty without dwelling in it. Say the hard thing
once, then move to what can be done about it.

## Banned phrasing

Phrases that mark a piece as machine-written, or as empty. They are banned outright, not
used sparingly.

**Stock openings and connectives**

- "In today's digital age"
- "Moreover"
- "Furthermore"
- "Delve into"
- "Harness the power of"
- "Unlock your potential"

**Journey and transformation language**

- "Navigate your ... journey"
- "Transform your ..." (any object: your finances, your future, your workflow, your life)

**Significance inflation**

- "This is not just X. It is Y."
- "This isn't only about X. It's about Y."

Do not tell the reader that a small thing is profound. If it is not significant, do not
inflate it.

## Quality test

Before anything is published, ask one question:

**Would Builders of MCP servers, agent tools and agent infrastructure read this and think "this was written for me"?**

If yes: publish.

If no: rewrite.
