# CONTENT

Core content craft. Always on, in every project that installs this kit.

Content craft is not voice. Voice lives in `VOICE.md` and applies everywhere. This file is
about how a piece of content is built: what it opens with, what it ends with, and what it
is allowed to claim.

Read this before writing a post, an article, a video script, a caption, an email, or a
landing page.

## Principles

**Open with the life moment, not the concept.** The situation that made the reader care
comes first. The idea comes second. Someone mid-life-moment is not looking for a concept.

**Make the reader feel recognised before they feel sold to.** Recognition comes first,
always. A reader who does not yet feel seen is not reading, they are scanning.

**End with one specific next step.** One, not a menu. It flows directly from what was just
learned and feels like a continuation, not an instruction. A next step is one thing a
person can do today, not a category of thing to think about.

**One number that surprises the reader.** Every piece contains at least one specific number
the reader did not expect. "You could save thousands" is not a number. A number with a
unit, a period and a comparison is.

**Make waiting visible.** A reader will always default to "I'll get to it". Make the cost
of that delay visible now, not in thirty years. Frame around present loss, not future
gain. Name the passive decision they are already making. Make the first action so small
it is easier to do than to refuse.

**Write for someone who is intelligent but not trained in the subject.** They can handle
complexity. They cannot handle unexplained jargon. They are neither stupid nor an
expert, and the copy has to work for both of those people at once.

**Sound like a person, not a platform.** No corporate voice, no "we are excited to
announce", no plural institutional first person where one real person is speaking.

## Headlines

Headline formulas are red ocean defaults. The headlines that work are the ones that name a
specific feeling or situation the reader is already having.

A headline that works:

- Opens with a feeling or life moment, not a concept.
- Contains a specific number or timeframe.
- Makes the reader think it was written for them specifically.
- Creates an information gap the reader needs to close.

Examples of the right territory:

- "You just got a pay rise. Here is the one thing to do before Friday."
- "Most people have no idea what they are actually paying for this. Here is how to find
  out."
- "The reason it costs more every year is that nobody ever showed you the alternative."
- "If you have been putting this off for a year, you are not behind. You are just
  uninformed."

Openings work the same way, whether it is a headline or the first three seconds of a
video.

Work:

- A question the viewer cannot comfortably ignore without finding out the answer.
- A contradiction that challenges something the viewer assumed was true.
- A specific life moment stated in the second person.

Do not work:

- Statements of intent: "Today I am going to talk about..."
- Generic openings that tell the reader what category they are in.
- Product-first openings, where the product arrives before the reader's problem.

## Do

- Use simple, direct language.
- Include specific numbers and real examples.
- Translate every piece of jargon the moment it appears. No term survives without a
  plain-English gloss.
- Consider search intent in every piece. People arrive with a sentence, not a keyword.
- Validate every internal link against the site map before publishing.
- Give every video captions. Most social video is watched without sound, and captions are
  an acquisition channel, not an accessibility afterthought.
- Keep one idea per piece. The reader should be able to describe what they learned in a
  single sentence.
- Vary sentence structure across the piece.
- Open with the reader's situation, not the product.

## Do not

- Do not open a video or article with the product. The product earns its appearance.
- Do not create content that could belong to any other product in the space. Every piece
  should be distinctly yours.
- Do not use fake testimonials, fabricated success stories, invented names, or made-up
  results. If an example is illustrative, say so.
- Do not use jargon without immediate plain-English explanation.
- Do not lead with a product feature.
- Do not describe the product as "AI-powered". Say what it does.
- Do not end on a menu of next steps.

## Visual storytelling

- One idea per visual.
- Typography is the primary visual element. If the words are not carrying the frame, the
  frame is wrong.
- Grid-based layouts only.
- If it does not serve the story, remove it.

## Motion

Two registers, chosen by context, never mixed.

**Short-form social.** Punchy and intentional. Word-by-word delivery where appropriate,
timed to create emphasis and rhythm. Motion feels direct and confident, not cinematic and
restrained.

**Longer-form and brand.** Minimal, intentional movement. Slow acceleration and soft
deceleration. Fewer elements moving at once. Transitions feel continuous rather than
cut-based. Allow moments of stillness.

Never apply the cinematic style to short-form social, and never apply the punchy style to
brand content.
